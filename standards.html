STANDARDS & LIMITATIONS

Clarity includes knowing what a visual can—and cannot—prove.

Scope, source material, assumptions, and qualifications matter as much as presentation quality.

Source transparency

Project materials should be inventoried and referenced. Where practical, exhibits should identify the source of relevant labels, measurements, timestamps, and scene details.

Facts, assumptions, and illustrations

Known source information, client-provided assumptions, disputed facts, and illustrative elements should not be presented as interchangeable. Unverified information should be flagged for review.

Not an automatic expert opinion

A diagram, animation, or demonstrative exhibit does not by itself establish speed, timing, visibility, impact dynamics, negligence, causation, or liability. Technical analyses and expert opinions require appropriate methods, data, qualifications, and a separately defined engagement.

Client review and intended use

The client is responsible for reviewing factual accuracy, confirming source materials, and determining suitability for a particular legal proceeding. Court admissibility is determined by the applicable court, not guaranteed by the format or provider.

Professional engagement

Before accepting a project, Clear Fault should disclose the actual personnel involved, relevant qualifications, scope of work, quality-control process, insurance status where applicable, and any limitations material to the engagement. Do not publish credentials or guarantees that cannot be substantiated.
